# Book of Proof (Hammack), 3rd edition

Source: `BookOfProof.pdf` (380 PDF pages). Free/CC-licensed edition.

## Plaintext scans

There are two, and **you almost always want `BookOfProof.txt`**.

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `BookOfProof.txt` | The readable scan. Generated by `mkscan.py`. Use this. |
| `BookOfProof.raw.txt` | Verbatim `pdftotext -enc UTF-8 -layout` output. Audit trail only. |

Regenerate either with:

```
python3 mkscan.py BookOfProof.pdf BookOfProof.txt
pdftotext -enc UTF-8 -layout BookOfProof.pdf BookOfProof.raw.txt
```

### Why the raw scan is unusable on its own

None of this book's fonts carry a ToUnicode map, and the math is set in the
Fourier family. Plain `pdftotext` therefore produces two kinds of damage:

- **Every math delimiter becomes an unrelated Latin-1 byte.** `{` `}` arrive
  as `©` `ª`, `|` as `¯`, `(` `)` as `¡` `¢`, and the braces routinely land
  on the line *above or below* their own content. Set-builder notation —
  the core notation of chapter 1 — is effectively destroyed.
- **Sub- and superscripts collapse onto the baseline and interleave.**
  $F_{n+1}^2$ comes out as `F n2+1`, which reads as a perfectly plausible
  but completely different expression. This one fails *silently*.

`mkscan.py` fixes both by reading `pdftohtml -xml`, which exposes the font
family and the size/position of every span. Provenance decides the ambiguous
glyphs (`;` is ∅ only when it came from the math font, never when it came
from the text font), and size plus vertical offset recovers the scripts.
Every glyph mapping in that script was verified against its context in the
book, not guessed.

### Notation used in `BookOfProof.txt`

- Scripts are explicit: `F_{n+1}^2`, `x^2`, `A_1`. Braces only when the
  script is longer than one character.
- Binomial coefficients are spelled out: `binom(n,k)`. (The book itself
  notes that some texts write `C(n,k)`; `binom` is used here because `C` is
  constantly in use as a set name.)
- Fractions are inlined with parentheses: `(1+√5)/2`, `(9!)/(4!5!)`.
- Recovered symbols: `∅ √ ′ ⌊⌋ ⌈⌉ 𝒫 ℱ ∑ ⋃ ⋂ ∤ ✓ ℓ ≠ ⊈ ≢`.
- `ℝ ℤ ℕ ℚ` are written as plain `R Z N Q`, as the book pronounces them.

### What is still lossy

Read the PDF (`Read` with `pages:`) when any of these matter:

- **Display fractions and stacked display math.** An inline `\frac` is
  recovered, but a large displayed one leaves numerator and denominator on
  separate lines with the rule missing.
- **Displayed binomials and summation limits** sometimes stack across lines
  rather than inlining; the inline forms are fine.
- **Figures, diagrams, Venn diagrams and the dice glyphs are drawn, not
  set as text.** They come through as blank space — e.g. Example 1.3's
  `A={  ,  ,  ,  ,  ,  }` is really six dice faces.
- **Radical extent is not marked.** `√x^2−x−1` does not record how far the
  bar reaches.
- **Complement overlines are drawn as rules and vanish entirely.** This one
  also fails silently: Definition 1.6's `A=U −A` is really `A̅ = U − A`, and
  Exercises 1.6 come through as nine indistinguishable copies of `A∩B`.
  Always read the PDF when complements are in play. A 900-dpi crop
  (`pdftoppm -r 900 -png -x .. -y .. -W .. -H ..`) is the reliable way to
  see which symbols a bar covers.
- Column alignment in wide tables can jitter by a space or two.

Truth tables — all of chapter 2 — come through with columns intact, and the
Solutions chapter is near-perfect prose.

## Page mapping

Pages are separated by form feeds (`\f`). The offset is constant, verified
on 342 of 380 pages (the rest are chapter openers, which carry no running
head):

```
PDF page = book page + 12
```

To print a book page:

```
python3 -c "print(open('BookOfProof.txt').read().split(chr(12))[BOOKPAGE+11])"
```

### The book contains its own solutions

Book pages 292–364 hold worked solutions to the **odd-numbered** exercises,
and that section extracts cleanly. Two consequences:

- When checking the user's work on an odd-numbered exercise, the book's own
  answer is available — use it to confirm, and say when your reasoning
  differs from Hammack's.
- Do not read ahead into it while setting an exercise for the user, and
  never paste it at them before they have answered.

## Conventions in this book

- `∼` is **negation** ("not P"), not equivalence. Hammack introduces it in
  section 2.2. Do not read it as a tilde relation.
- Results are numbered per chapter as `Definition 3.2`, `Fact 1.1`,
  `Theorem 10.1`, `Example 1.4` — separate sequences per kind, unlike the
  single continuous sequence Axler uses in LADR. Cite by kind and number.
- `∅` is written `;` in the raw scan and `∅` in the clean one. If you ever
  find yourself reading the raw scan, remember that some `;` really are
  semicolons — 102 of the 349 are.
- Sections marked `∗` are flagged by the author as skippable.

## Conventions in our own `chNN/` files

The scan writes `R Z N Q`, but our `definitions.md` and `exercises.md` use
the book's own blackboard bold `ℝ ℤ ℕ ℚ`, and `𝒫` for the power set.

Set complement is written with a combining overline: `A̅`. When the bar
covers a compound expression, write it as `‾(A ∩ B)`. Both files state this
convention in their header.
